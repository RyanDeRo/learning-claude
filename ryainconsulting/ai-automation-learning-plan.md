# AI Consulting for Local Businesses — Learning Plan (rewritten 2026-11-20)

Goal: get good enough to **sell and deliver AI help to local businesses** (tattoo shops, bakeries, nail salons, smoothie bars, trades, real estate agents) and explain it in plain English.

This plan starts from first principles: what a local business actually runs on and where AI really helps. It does **not** borrow from enterprise IT (monitoring, incident response, observability). A bakery doesn't have a cloud environment to watch. Anything that only makes sense at enterprise scale doesn't belong here.

How to use it: work through it in order. Each module ends with a **rep**: real work with a real business (your own or an existing client) that becomes a demo, case study or testimonial. Log progress and lessons in `journal.md`. Tool prices and features change fast, so check current ones when you reach each module instead of trusting memory (including mine).

---

## First principles

**1. What a local business runs on.** Every one does the same four jobs:
- **Get found and get the inquiry:** Google Business Profile, website, Instagram, word of mouth, calls/DMs/forms.
- **Book and serve:** scheduling, quotes, reminders, the actual work.
- **Get paid and do the admin:** invoices, payments, bookkeeping, ordering supplies, staff schedules.
- **Keep customers coming back:** reviews, follow-ups, social posts, specials.

**2. The owner's scarcest resource is time, not technology.** They're usually the one doing the work *and* answering the phone *and* doing the books at 10pm. The pitch is "get your evenings back" or "stop losing customers you never called back," never "use AI."

**3. Where AI genuinely helps a small business is words.** Writing, replying, summarizing, rewriting, translating, brainstorming: social captions, review replies, quote emails, menu/service descriptions, policies, job posts, answering the same 10 questions. That's most of the real value.

**4. Most of what they need isn't AI at all.** Reminders, booking links, auto-replies and review-request texts are plain automation, and most of it is **already built into tools they pay for** (Square, Vagaro, Google, Meta Business Suite). Often the job is turning on and setting up what they already own. That's cheaper, more reliable, and something they can keep running without you.

**5. It has to survive without you.** If the owner can't understand it or keep it running, it's a liability. Simple beats clever every time.

## Module 1 — Learn the businesses (discovery)

The most important knowledge to gain isn't about tools. It's how local businesses actually spend their day.

**Learn:**
- A 20–30 minute interview: "Walk me through yesterday. What do you do every week that you hate? Where do customers slip through the cracks? What do you answer over and over? What software do you already pay for?"
- How to spot a good opportunity: happens often, eats real time or loses real money, low risk if it goes wrong.

**Rep:** Interview 3–5 owners you already know: Kadillac Tattoo, the nail salons, the smoothie shop, Personal Touch Maintenance, your BJJ gym. Write one page per business listing the top 3 time sinks. Patterns across them tell you what to sell.

## Module 2 — AI for the owner's writing work ("AI best use")

**Learn:**
- What chat AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) is good at (drafting, rewriting, summarizing, explaining) and bad at (facts it can't check, math, anything that must be right without a human read-through).
- Prompting that transfers: give context about the business, show an example, name the format and tone, ask it to ask questions first.
- Saving work: custom instructions, Projects / custom GPTs loaded with the business's info, a short library of reusable prompts.
- AI already inside their tools (Google Workspace, Canva, Square, Meta).
- **What not to paste:** customer personal info, card numbers, health details. Free vs. paid plan privacy settings. Every owner will ask "is this safe?"

**Rep:** Set up an assistant for one real business (start with ryAIn itself, then a client): their services, prices, policies, voice. Add 5 saved prompts they'd actually use, such as an Instagram caption, a review reply, an answer to a common question, a quote email and a weekly special. Time one task before and after.
**Sellable output:** "AI set up for your business": a 90-minute session, their own tool, a one-page cheat sheet.

## Module 3 — Get more out of the tools they already pay for

**Learn** the platforms your target businesses actually use, and what each can do on its own:
- **Google Business Profile:** posts, Q&A, messaging, review replies (you already sell this).
- **Booking/POS:** Square (Appointments, Marketing), Vagaro/Fresha/GlossGenius (salons), etc. Automatic reminders, confirmations, review requests, rebooking nudges.
- **Meta Business Suite:** Instagram/Facebook auto-replies, saved replies, scheduled posts.
- **Google Workspace / Gmail:** templates, filters, shared inbox basics.

**Rep:** For one client, audit the tools they already have and turn on 2–3 features they're paying for but not using (e.g. booking reminders + automatic review request). Measure a before/after: no-shows, reviews per month, hours saved.
**Sellable output:** a "tools tune-up". Cheap to deliver, obvious value, no new monthly bills for the client.

## Module 4 — Never miss a customer (inquiries and follow-up)

**Learn:**
- Missed-call text-back and instant inquiry replies: how they work and which simple tools do it.
- **US texting rules:** A2P 10DLC registration and TCPA consent for business texting. Learn this before selling anything that texts customers.
- Connecting tools when built-ins can't do it: one no-code platform (Zapier or Make; pick one) for jobs like "website form → text the owner + log it in a sheet."
- When an AI step is worth adding (e.g. drafting a reply to an inquiry for the owner to approve) and when it's not.

**Rep:** Set up missed-call text-back on a test number for ryAIn. It's your 60-second in-person demo. Then hook one client's website form up to an instant reply.
**Sellable output:** the ~$100–$200/mo "never miss an inquiry" add-on from `market-research.md`.

## Module 5 — Packaging, pricing and handoff

**Learn:**
- Fixed-price, clearly scoped offers beat hourly for owners who fear surprise bills.
- Write down what's included, what isn't, and how the owner handles things without you.
- Handoff: the client owns every account, has a one-page "how this works" sheet, and knows when to call you.
- Showing value: a short monthly note in the owner's language ("you got 12 new reviews; 4 missed calls got a text back and 2 booked").

**Rep:** Turn the best result from Modules 2–4 into a one-page offer with a price, and pitch it to one client.

## Later, only as needed — agents, MCP, custom builds
Know what "AI agent" and "connectors/MCP" mean well enough to explain them in a sentence. You use them yourself in Claude Code. Local owners rarely need them, so don't lead with them.

## Suggested pace (side-job hours)

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Module 1: owner interviews (start now; it shapes everything else) |
| 2–3 | Module 2: AI writing assistant, first for ryAIn, then one client |
| 4–5 | Module 3: tools tune-up for one client |
| 6–7 | Module 4: missed-call demo + one form auto-reply, plus texting rules |
| 8 | Module 5: package and price the best result, pitch it |

## How you'll know you're ready to sell it
- You've interviewed enough owners to name the 3 problems that come up every time.
- You have 2–3 working demos you can show on your phone.
- You can answer "is my customer data safe?" and "can I legally text my customers?" correctly.
- One owner has said it saved them time or money, in words you can quote.

## Honest take
- **Strongest:** tools tune-ups and "never miss an inquiry" for clients whose website you already run. Trust is there, the ROI is easy to see, and it's low maintenance.
- **Good:** "AI set up for your business" sessions, as long as they're specific to that business. Generic AI training competes with free workshops from chambers, Google and SBDCs; teach those for free as lead gen instead.
- **Avoid for now:** anything that needs you to respond live during your day job, or that the owner couldn't keep running without you.
